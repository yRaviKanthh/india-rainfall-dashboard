Academic Methodology & Data Processing Notes

1. Data Cleaning & Imputation:

The 1901–2015 dataset contains 4,116 records. Out of 49,392 monthly values, 70 missing entries (0.14%) were detected and imputed using the specific subdivision's long-term monthly mean. Seasonal aggregates (Winter: Jan-Feb, Pre-Monsoon: Mar-May, Monsoon: Jun-Sep, Post-Monsoon: Oct-Dec) and annual totals were mathematically recalculated to ensure exact additive consistency.

2. Trend Analysis Classification:

Trends were determined using Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) linear regression of annual precipitation against year (1901–2015). Regions with absolute slope |m| ≥ 0.5 mm/year were classified as Increasing or Decreasing, while those with |m| < 0.5 mm/year were classified as Stable climatological regimes.

3. Predictive Modeling & Evaluation:

Next-year (2016) forecasts were generated via three methodologies: (1) OLS Linear Regression, (2) 5-Year Moving Average (recent short-term mean), and (3) 10-Year Moving Average (decadal mean). Out-of-sample one-step-ahead backtesting was performed across the historical series to compute Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) for each model, highlighting the empirical best performer.

4. Climatological Normals & Baseline:

District normals from district_wise_rainfall_normal.csv (641 districts across 35 States/UTs) corroborate the sub-divisional long-period averages (LPA), identifying intra-state microclimatic extremes (e.g., Cherrapunji/Mawsynram in East Khasi Hills, Meghalaya vs Jaisalmer, Rajasthan).
